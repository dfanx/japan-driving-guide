## Red means stop, including turns

You reach a red light in Japan and the direction you want to turn appears clear of vehicles and pedestrians.

Stop at the stopping point. Do not turn left or right on your own judgment.

## Three moments in a right turn

1. **Circular green:** You may enter to prepare for the turn, but you do not have priority. Yield to opposing straight and left-turning traffic and to pedestrians or cyclists crossing the road you will enter.
2. **Green right arrow:** You may turn in the arrow direction even while the main light is red. It removes much of the opposing straight-traffic conflict, but you must still scan for people and traffic that has not cleared.
3. **Already waiting inside when the signal changes:** If you lawfully entered on green, complete the turn promptly once opposing traffic has stopped and without obstructing the newly released traffic. There is no fixed “two-to-three-second right.” If red appears while you are still behind the stopping position, do not enter.

## Yellow does not mean accelerate

Stop before the line when you can do so smoothly and safely. Continue only when you are already too close to stop safely; do not accelerate to beat the signal.

## Flashing red means stop; flashing yellow means caution

At flashing red, make a complete stop and check before proceeding. At flashing yellow, slow and proceed with care. Flashing yellow does not prove that you are on the major road or give absolute priority.

## A long red at a quiet side road may be vehicle-actuated

Stop immediately before the stop line where the detector can sense the vehicle, then wait. Stopping too far back or creeping beyond the line may keep the vehicle outside the detection zone. Follow any local button or instruction panel.

## A yellow arrow is not for cars

On streets with trams, a yellow arrow permits the streetcar only. Cars and motorcycles must continue to obey their own circular light or green arrow.

## Common mistake

Importing a turn-on-red habit from another country into Japan.
