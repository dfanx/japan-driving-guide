# F034 local driving customs research

Checked: 2026-08-12

## Editorial boundary

The reported behaviours are useful only when a visitor can distinguish an
observed custom from a legal signal. Each item below is accepted, narrowed, or
rejected before entering a lesson.

| Reported claim | Decision | Published teaching |
|---|---|---|
| Always switch on hazards before a temporary stop or reverse park | Narrow | Slow early and use the legally required directional signal where applicable. Hazards are a common supplementary cue for stopping or reverse parking, but are not universal and never replace mirrors, blind-spot checks, brake/reverse lights, or a spotter. |
| Two hazard flashes mean thank you | Narrow | Many drivers may read a brief flash as thanks, but it is an unofficial, ambiguous courtesy. Use it only after the manoeuvre is complete and never treat it as permission or priority. |
| Heavy traffic merges one vehicle at a time | Accept as official guidance | NEXCO Central asks drivers in congestion to continue to the end of the merge lane and alternate one vehicle at a time. This is taught as zipper merging, subject to signs, markings and safe gaps. |
| Never change lanes inside an intersection | Narrow | Japan has no blanket nationwide rule making every intersection lane change illegal. Choose the lane before entry and normally hold it through the conflict area; obey yellow boundaries, lane arrows and local controls. |
| Keep within posted limit plus or minus 15 km/h | Reject the number | No official source creates a `+15 km/h` allowance. Above the limit is speeding. A limit is not a target; reduce speed for conditions without needlessly obstructing traffic, and obey any minimum-speed control. |
| Follow the vehicle ahead through a railway crossing if no train is visible | Reject | Each vehicle stops and checks for itself, and does not enter until the entire exit is clear. The Road Traffic Act includes a signal-controlled crossing exception, which the lesson states explicitly rather than claiming there are no exceptions. |

## Source evidence

- S31 — NEXCO Central, [Expressway Safety and Manners](https://www.c-nexco.co.jp/safety/safety_drive/manner/manner_guide/03.html): congestion zipper merging at the lane end, one vehicle at a time.
- S32 — Panasonic Insurance Services, [Hazard-lamp safety note](https://panasonic.co.jp/pisj/useful/car/tasuke2110.php): distinguishes statutory hazard uses from other communication and warns that stopping/deceleration use is not generally prescribed.
- S33 — Traffic Science Society paper, [Traffic communication research](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/kokaken/52/1/52_02/_pdf): identifies reverse-parking and thank-you hazards as unofficial signals and reports lower understanding among novice groups.
- S16/S29 — current Road Traffic Act and NPA traffic-method guide: posted maximum speed, safe speed/method, required signalling and intersection risk.
- S24/S28 — police sources: hazards do not change abandoned-parking status; yellow solid lane boundaries control lane changes.

## Roadside-facility right-turn entry clarification

The NPA driving guide states that a driver may turn right to leave the road after moving toward the road centre and slowing, unless crossing or turning is prohibited; the manoeuvre must not obstruct pedestrians or the normal movement of other traffic. The learner copy therefore teaches this order:

1. Read official road signs and markings, including any vehicle-crossing or mandatory-direction control.
2. Read the facility entrance notice, such as `右折入庫禁止`.
3. If no restriction is present, treat the turn as potentially permitted—not automatically safe—and yield to opposing traffic and sidewalk users.
4. If visibility is poor or the car would wait sideways across traffic, continue and re-approach from the same direction at a lawful safe location.

This avoids two opposite errors: assuming every right-turn entry is forbidden, and assuming the absence of a facility notice guarantees a safe turn.
