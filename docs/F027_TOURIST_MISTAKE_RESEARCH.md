# F027 Tourist-Mistake Research — 2026-08-11

## Purpose

The six user-supplied travel and rental articles are discovery inputs: they show
which questions visitors repeatedly ask and which explanations are easy to
understand. They are not promoted to legal Source IDs. Every production legal
claim is checked against NPA, e-Gov, police, MLIT, JAF, or NEXCO material.

## Gaps accepted into F027

- Explain the visual and legal difference between `駐車禁止` (one red slash)
  and `駐停車禁止` (red X).
- Kill the “just two minutes” myth: leaving the vehicle unable to move it
  immediately is not rescued by a short duration, and hazard lights do not make
  an illegal stop legal.
- Give tourists a compact distance memory aid: intersections/crossings use a
  five-metre clear zone; railway crossings and operating bus/tram stops use ten
  metres. The lesson points readers to the official diagram for the full list.
- Add distracted-driving, all-seat-belt, under-six child-seat, and alcohol-
  assistance rules to the safety baseline.
- Turn the Fast Track page into a real ten-item pre-pickup checklist instead of
  a stale one-lesson teaser.
- Replace internal/editorial language such as “approved path”, “production-
  ready”, and “judgment feedback” with concise Taiwan-facing instructions.

## Claims deliberately rejected or narrowed

- A secondary page says phone use is prohibited even while stopped at a red
  light. Road Traffic Act Article 71 excludes a vehicle while stopped; the site
  instead gives the safer practical instruction to set navigation only after
  pulling into a lawful safe place.
- A secondary page suggests reversing after entering the wrong road when safe.
  F027 does not publish this advice; learners are told not to enter against the
  sign and to stop safely and seek police/help if an error creates danger.
- “One beer always exceeds the limit”, fixed fine amounts, and insurance/NOC
  outcomes vary by facts or contract. They are not presented as universal rules.
- Courtesy hazard flashes and horn etiquette are not elevated into legal rules.

## Voice rule

Write like a careful Taiwanese friend who has driven in Japan: lead with the
mistake, give one concrete action, then explain only the exception the learner
needs. Keep legal terms when precision matters, but translate them immediately.
No hype, no “comprehensive solution”, no editorial workflow language in public
copy.
